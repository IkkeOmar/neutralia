Balancing rules

R1–R3 and R7 come from Battlefield 2042's own published failure modes: after launch, DICE reported community feedback about "the prevalence of explosive weaponry, and unclear combat roles across vehicles", and reworked every vehicle loadout to fix it. R4–R5 come from Battlefield 1, which shipped four chassis with three variants each and changed seat count per variant.

Invariants

The simulator found two real design flaws on its first run and both were fixed in the design rather than loosened in the assertion: contested role lines (two variants of one chassis fighting the same battle) and a Scout car with no predator.

Deferred — Honor

Level-gated PvP is parked for the future. "Leveling can also be power, not only choice … restricting which levels can fight against which levels (we can call it 'honor')."

It is not separable from level brackets: if level carries vertical power, brackets become mandatory or a level 30 deletes a level 10 in the open world. Precedent exists — World of Warcraft's Honor ranks with 10–19 / 20–29 / … / 60 battleground brackets, and Rainbow Six Siege's Skill-versus-Rank split with squad restrictions.

Nothing in the vehicle system depends on it, so the deferral costs nothing.

Original roster — measured

"Each faction has access to four vehicles: jeeps, medium tanks, fighter planes, and helicopters."
Battlefield Heroes, Battlefield Wiki
Vehicles spawned only at controlled points, and 5 of BFH's 14 maps had no vehicles at all. The Jeep was on 6 maps — the most ubiquitous chassis, which is why our rule R7 caps it at the lowest power ceiling.
