Weapon Specification System

Companion to GAME-DESIGN.md. Implements pillar P1 — weapon choice must be functional.

All numbers below are drawn from the actual Battlefield Heroes data (scraped from battlefield.fandom.com and bfh-info.cs-heroes.cz on 2026-11-07; raw data in research/02-weapon-roster.json and research/wt_Weapon_Variants.txt).

1. The problem this solves — measured, not asserted

The original Battlefield Heroes shipped 346 weapons. The breakdown, from the scraped roster:

Category Count Class
SMG 64 Soldier
Rifle 64 Commando
MG 62 Gunner
Pistol 44 Soldier
Grenade Launcher 40 Soldier + Gunner
Shotgun 40 Soldier + Gunner
Knife 22 Commando
Sticky TNT 6 Soldier
Grenade 4 Soldier

346 weapons. Nine functional archetypes. And the arithmetic is worse than "9 × 5".

Measured against the scraped roster and stat detail:

  • 185 base weapons, 161 tier variants (37 Super, 37 Uber, 21 Stolen, 21 Pilfered, 13 Dapper, 13 Stylish, 16 Golden).
  • The 185 base weapons share their stat profiles heavily: only 55 distinct 4-axis stat profiles across all 185 (range, rate of fire, ammo, damage), so only 7 weapons have a unique profile. 34 grenade launchers collapse to 5 — M32 MGL and Tank Buster differ only in magazine count. Across the rest: 29 SMGs → 11 profiles, 29 MGs → 8, 31 rifles → 10, 22 pistols → 9, 18 shotguns → 7, 12 knives → 1.
  • But they are not interchangeable. The same 185 weapons carry 95 distinct behaviour descriptions. Tank Buster is a "long range anti-vehicle weapon"; M32 MGL is a "bulky grenade launcher... great for laying down areas of infantry destruction". The Knife family is the extreme case — 12 knives, one stat profile, 7 distinct behaviours (Sensei's Striking Edge: "slow but long and powerful"; Dirk's Digger: "for digging graves").

The tier system on top:

Tier Stat effect Cost Release
Base — Valor / Play4Free Funds launch
Dapper (Roy) / Stylish (Nat) statistically identical, cosmetic only Battlefunds / P4F Heroes of the Fall
Super / Uber "statistically and cosmetically different... featuring minor stat changes" Battlefunds / VP / P4F Winter Armistice
Stolen / Pilfered identical, but the enemy's weapon Battlefunds 19 May 2010
Golden premium — later

Faction split is exactly 173 / 173. Class split: Soldier 178, Commando 86, Gunner 82.

This is the actual design problem, stated in numbers. The original reskinned itself twice: 185 base weapons occupying 55 stat profiles but 95 distinct behaviours, then 161 cosmetic-and-minor-stat variants on top. Weapon choice was therefore mostly cosmetic choice, and the "minor stat changes" on Super/Uber were paid upgrades — a pay-to-win layer bolted onto a reskin farm. Precisely the model that cannot survive into an MMO where horizontal progression is the whole point.

(Correction, 2026-11-07: an earlier draft of this paragraph said the base weapons were "functionally ~25" and were "near-duplicates". The measured behaviour data shows that was too strong — 95 distinct behaviours across 185 weapons is not 25. The stat-clone ratios are real and measured; the interchangeability claim was wrong. See systems/class-and-weapon-families.md §2.1.)

The pivot: keep the 9 archetypes (they are good — 3 classes × primary + secondary), and make the depth per archetype the content instead of the count.

Follow-on (2026-11-07): the class×family re-cut and the fourth class now live in systems/class-and-weapon-families.md. The 9 archetypes are re-cut into 11 families across 4 classes, and the roster target drops to 110 base weapons — 10 per family, each required to differ on ≥3 of 6 axes.

1b. What the original already had: the Workbench

Added 2026-11-07. An earlier draft of this document claimed the original "had no way to express" a behaviour-only weapon option. That was wrong. Battlefield Heroes shipped exactly that system — and it shipped it last, in December 2012, as a late content patch.

The Workbench (introduced 5 December 2012) lets you modify any weapon you own permanently. Each weapon has a limited number of upgrade slots, and only one upgrade of each type can be equipped at a time — equipping a second of the same type deletes the first. Three categories:

1. Upgrades (mostly flat stats, per weapon class): Super Packin' (MG ammo +10), Packin' I–III (ammo +2/+3/+4, crit +1 %/+1 %/+1 %, crit damage +1/+1/+2), Fingers Crossed I–III (crit), Come Closer I–III (short-range distance), Just Right I–III (short-range min. damage, with range falloff), Rifle Focus I–III (recoil), Highly Explosive I–III (explosion damage/radius/force), Go Away! I–III (pushback), Super Crit, Standard Scope (equips a scope but blocks the secondary-fire slot).

2. Secondary Fire — this is the important one. These are new behaviours bolted onto the weapon: | Secondary Fire | Effect | Applies to | |---|---|---| | Explosive Shot | Adds a burst of explosive firepower | MGs | | Quick Fire | Raises the weapon's rate of fire | MGs | | Push Beam | Fires a beam that pushes enemies away | SMGs | | Underslug Grenade Launcher | Equips the weapon with a grenade launcher | SMGs, Shotguns | | Shotgun Attachment | Equips the sidearm with a shotgun | Sidearms | | Laser Beam / Laser Charge / Punk Shotgun / Energy Ball | Replaces the firing mode entirely | various |

3. Special Features — conditional behaviours, not stats: | Special Feature | Effect | |---|---| | Dr. Bullet | Regain 5 HP every 3rd critical hit | | Berserk | Deal 17 % more damage at 20 HP or below | | Explosion Demotion | Take 20 % less explosion damage | | Meep meep! | Run faster at 30 HP or below (scales with Leg It) | | Heavy 'n Steady | Take 35 % less explosion force | | I Feel Lucky | +5 % crit chance, but −2 min damage at short and medium range |

I Feel Lucky is the exact principle this document was built on, shipped in 2009-era code: a real trade — you gain crit, you lose flat damage — expressed as a gamble the player opts into, because it narrows their effective band.

What the Workbench gets right, and what it does not:

  • Right: per-weapon identity. Push Beam on an SMG makes that SMG a different weapon. Meep meep! is a real playstyle. One upgrade per type forces a coherent build instead of a pile of stacking bonuses.
  • Wrong: it is a slot-swap grid, not a tree. No progression path, no branching, no depth. You either own the upgrade or you do not. There is no earning a signature; you buy it — and it was priced in Play4Free Funds, i.e. real money.
  • Wrong: the upgrade slots were not standardised, so the best build was whichever weapon happened to have the most slots.

So the pivot stands, but it is now sharper and better founded. The original shipped a flat version of this system two years in and then never built depth on top of it. Our contribution is not "per-weapon upgrades exist" — the original did that — it is that they become a branching tree earned through use, with a behaviour-only Signature at the end that cannot be bought.

2. The six axes

Every weapon is defined across six axes. A weapon is valid only if it differs from every other weapon in the same archetype by at least three.

Axis What it controls Range in this design
DPS profile How damage arrives burst 3×100 / sustained 8×25 / spike 1×250
Range Effective distance, falloff shape sharp falloff past 40m / flat to 90m / short-range only
Mobility Move speed, sprint, ADS time fast strafe + slow ADS / slow + instant ADS
Handling Recoil, spread, reload, swap high recoil + fast reload / low recoil + slow swap
Utility Secondary effects slow / reveal / mark / deny / heal / resupply
Team function The role it enables solo-ko, team-stabilize, zone-control, objective-hold

Anti-domination check. Before a weapon ships it must be compared against the two weapons it most resembles. If it wins on all six axes, it is cut. If it wins on four, one win must be paid for on one of the other two.

Functional floor. A weapon's category is defined by what it does, not what it looks like. A "rifle" that cannot contest mid-range is not a rifle in this system regardless of its model.

3. The per-weapon tree

Each owned weapon has its own tree. Shared currency (Valor, earned by playing) buys nodes. Short tree, meaningful branch.

        [Node 1]              cost 1
             |
        [Node 2] ──┬── [Node 3a] ──┐   cost 2 for the branch
                    └── [Node 3b] ──┤
                                    ├── [Node 4] ── [Node 5]      cost 1 each
                                    └── [Node 4′]
        [Signature tier 1]   cost 1  — pick one of three
        [Signature tier 2]   cost 1  — pick one of three
        [Signature tier 3]   cost 1  — pick one of three
                                    TOTAL = 8 points per weapon
  • Node 1 — small mechanical shift. Always a straight upgrade of an existing behaviour.
  • Node 2 — the branch point. Two mutually exclusive paths, each pushing one axis hard in one direction. This is where identity starts.
  • Node 3a / 3b — commit to a path. Respec allowed for Valor.
  • Node 4 / 4′ — role shift. A mid-range shooter becomes either an objective-holding tool or a pure duelist.
  • Node 5 — capstone: a change that interacts with other systems (a suppressor that also cuts spotting radius; a shotgun that also breaks vehicle tracks).
  • Signature tiers 1–3 — three separate pick-one-of-three choices, each unlocking a further permanent change to what the weapon does.

Weapon Skill Points accrue in combat. Per-weapon use XP, so levelling a weapon means playing that weapon — not clicking a button, and not leveling your character. The tree itself is spent out of combat at a bench, and the whole tree respecs cheaply and freely up to the Signature.

The Signature is the load-bearing node

A Signature must be a behavioural change, not a stat. A new fire mode, a new interaction, a new rule about what the weapon can do — not "+8% damage".

Name the behaviour, not the bonus. Tank Stars names its weapons Volley, Vertical Slam, Splitter Chain, Napalm Strike — almost none of them describe a number, because a player reading "Vertical Slam" understands what they will be doing at the 30 % mark (it fires up to 3 guided rockets that loop in the air and track a target they fly over). The original Battlefield Heroes' Workbench Special Features follow the same discipline: Dr. Bullet, Berserk, Meep meep!, I Feel Lucky — none of them is a number. Our Signatures follow the same rule: Executioner, Phantom, Overwatch, Interceptor, Saboteur. If a Signature name can be replaced with "+8 % damage" without anyone noticing, the name failed.

It is offered pick-one-of-three. This is the single most important interaction decision in the system. The source is Deep Rock Galactic: a weapon Overclock is normally rolled at drop time, but a Blank Matrix Core lets the player choose — 2 weapon options and 1 cosmetic — so the choice becomes the memory and the grind is only the setup. (Not Worms: an earlier draft of this document attributed a random "special effect" upgrade tier to the Worms Weapon Factory. That system does not exist — Worms 4's factory is a budgeted custom-weapon builder with an overheat gauge, and the Forts Weapon Factory is a crate-producing building. Checked against the primary wikis on 2026-11-07.) When a rare drop happens, offering three Signature options turns a lottery into a choice, and the moment becomes owned rather than won — the player remembers choosing, not getting lucky. (Deep Rock Galactic's Blank Core lesson.)

It binds, but respecs for a non-trivial yet affordable cost. Weight without a trap. Fully free respec makes the choice meaningless; hard-lock traps people in a bad pick after a balance change and turns the "meaningful choice" into a resentful sunk cost (Shadowlands Covenants). Our rule: the Signature respecs for 1 500 Valor — two hours of play, never a trap.

Why three tiers rather than one. A single Signature is a single decision, and after a month of play every player has made it and never thinks about it again. Three tiers means the weapon keeps asking the player a question, and the weapon's identity evolves over the first three months of ownership rather than being fixed on day one.

Total investment per weapon: 8 points. With 8 weapons owned, mastering the set costs 64 points — and points are earned per weapon by playing it. A player who spreads playtime across the set masters none of them. A player who commits masters exactly one.

(The first draft of this document granted weapon points from character level, which let a maxed player fully specialize 3–5 weapons and made the whole tree optional. The numbers were run and the design was corrected. tools/simulate-design.py now asserts this invariant, and prototype/test-prototype.js asserts it in the live DOM.)

4. The three real BFH classes, as they actually were

Extracted from the wikitext, not invented. Health and ability counts are the original's.

Soldier — 110 HP, "combines aspects of the Assault and Medic kits"

Weapons: SMG (primary, 64), Pistol (44), Shotgun (40), GL (20), Grenade (4), Sticky TNT (6)

Ability Actual effect
Sixth Sense You and your team see all nearby enemies for a short time
Burning Bullets SMG shots set enemies on fire, damage over time (extinguished by Combat Medicine)
Combat Medicine Instantly heals player and nearby allies; amount scales with number healed
Grenade Spam Throws multiple grenades at a target
Blasting Strike Strikes with such force that nearby players and vehicles are thrown aside

Note Combat Medicine scales with player count — it was already a cooperative ability in 2009. That is a gift to the MMO design: the class fantasy was already team-scale, not solo-sustain.

Gunner — 150 HP (highest), "the go-to class for Anti-Vehicle Warfare"

Weapons: MG (primary, 62), GL (20)

Ability Actual effect
Leg It Increases movement speed of player and nearby allies
Frenzy Fire Improves MG accuracy to focus a single target; heals a % of health every 4th hit
Explosive Keg Hurls a large explosive barrel that can be shot to explode early; causes enemies hit to run slower
Hero Shield Absorbs damage for player and nearby allies for a few seconds
I Eats Grenades Gobbles up all nearby explosives for a short time; each grenade eaten recovers health

Commando — 80 HP (weakest), "can do the most damage per hit", fastest infantry

Weapons: Rifle (primary, 64), Knife (22)

Ability Actual effect
Stealth Cloaking from enemy view
Elixir Increases speed and defense for a short time
Mark Target Receive points for designating enemy targets for your team; can reveal enemy Commandos using Stealth
Troop Trap Place an explosive that triggers when enemies approach too close or when it is shot
Piercing Shot Increases damage done by sniper rifles
Poisoned Blade Knife damage poisons the victim, losing health over time

Two design golds in the original, already present: 1. Mark Target / Sixth Sense — team information as an ability. The Commando earns points for marking; the Soldier reveals for the whole team. Information warfare was native to the class kit. 2. Poisoned Blade / Burning Bullets — damage-over-time as a weapon property, not a status effect bolted on. The knife and the SMG have their own identity.

4b. Worked examples

Illustrative. Final numbers come from the roster research in research/02-bfh-system-inventory.md.

4b.1 National Army assault rifle — "StG 44"

Base: 24 damage, 600 RPM, 4-round mag, moderate falloff past 45m, 4.2 m/s strafe.

Node Axis Change
1 Handling −25% vertical recoil
2 branch Marksmanship → Close-quarters
3a Range ×1.4 effective range, falloff starts at 70m
3b Handling ×1.8 ADS speed, −15% damage
4a Team function Marks kills reveal the target for 2s to teammates
4b DPS profile Sustained fire becomes burst-fire, ×2.2 burst damage
5 Utility Suppressed fire: no tracers, −40% audio detection radius
Signature A — Executioner: a kill instantly refreshes 25% of magazine
Signature B — Sapper: kills drop a 15-second ammo crate
Signature C — Ghost: standing still for 3s grants full concealment for 1 shot
Signature D (L30) — Marksman: every 5th kill grants +20% movement speed for 3s

Signature D is the model to copy. It is not a bigger number — it is a new interaction (kills feed mobility) that changes how the weapon is played: the SMG becomes a hit-and-move weapon rather than a static spray. All four signatures change behaviour, never just damage.

4b.2 Royal Army sniper rifle — "Lee-Enfield"

Base: 85 damage, 45 RPM, 5-round mag, very sharp falloff past 120m, slow ADS.

Node Axis Change
1 Range −10% falloff beyond 150m
2 branch Stability → Speed
3a Handling −40% recoil, +1 zoom step
3b DPS profile ×1.6 fire rate, −15% damage
4a Utility Hits mark the target (shows on all team displays)
4b Mobility +12% movement speed while scoped, −25% while unscoped
5 Team function Destroying a vehicle with this weapon awards a squad-wide damage buff
Signature A — Overwatch: the first shot after 2s unscoped deals ×2
Signature B — Spotter: marks last for 8s and shows enemy count, not positions
Signature C — Saboteur: +30% damage to vehicles
Signature D (L30) — Marksman: a marked target takes +15% damage from all sources

Spotter and Marksman build on the original's own Mark Target ability — the Commando's kit already had team information as a mechanic. The Signature system extends what the game already did well.

4b.3 Commando SMG — suppressed "MP40"

Base: 14 damage, 900 RPM, 30-round mag, short range, fast movement.

This weapon's identity is information denial, not damage. Its tree pushes utility and mobility, and its signatures remove the enemy team's advantage rather than dealing more damage:

  • Signature A — Phantom: while not firing for 4s, the player appears as a friendly dot on enemy minimaps.
  • Signature B — Interceptor: destroying enemy deployables (sandbags, ammo crates) grants +40% movement speed for 4s.
  • Signature C — Saboteur: destroyed enemy deployables drop the enemy's own valor.

Signature A is the strongest idea in this document. It is purely about information, with zero damage attached, and it inverts the enemy's scouting advantage rather than out-shooting anyone.

Corrected 2026-11-07: an earlier draft said this "could not exist in the original's model". That is no longer defensible — the Workbench (see §1b) could attach a Special Feature to any weapon, and the original already had information-warfare abilities (Sixth Sense reveals for the whole team; Mark Target counters stealth). What the original could not do was make a signature earned and permanent — Workbench upgrades were bought with real money and applied as slot swaps, with no progression. The gap is in the economy and the depth, not in the expressive range. That is a more useful and more honest framing, and it is also a better argument for the design: we are not inventing a capability, we are making the capability earned, branchable, and paid for with play.

5. Three specs per class

WoW pre-Mists-of-Pandaria style, reworked. Each class has three specializations. Choosing a branch at level 10 commits 4 of 6 subsequent Class Points to it; 2 stay free.

Class Spec A Spec B Spec C
Soldier Medic — scales Combat Medicine, revives, deployable cover Demolitions — satchel charges, mines, breach; scales Blasting Strike Marksman — SMG at rifle range, scales Sixth Sense into long-range spotting
Gunner Heavy — Frenzy Fire focus, sustained MG, scales Hero Shield Anti-Tank — GL/Rocket specialisation, vehicle killing, scales Explosive Keg Sapper — I Eats Grenades ordnance play, repair, area denial
Commando Recon — Stealth, Troop Trap, spotting, sabotage Sniper — Piercing Shot, long range, marked kills Infiltrator — Poisoned Blade, unlock, supply theft, speed

The Commando split is deliberate. In the original, Commando was a single sniper archetype with 6 abilities covering stealth, marking and knives all at once — a class carrying three identities and none of them deeply. Splitting it into Recon / Sniper / Infiltrator creates three viable team compositions from one base class.

6. Field promotion (in-match)

Separate from the persistent tree. In-match valor buys temporary upgrades from 3 tokens carried on the player. Tokens are lost on death.

Cost Upgrade Effect
50 Rearm Refill magazine, no reload animation
100 Overcharge +25% damage to the next 8 shots
150 Marksman (temporary) Reveal all enemies within 60m for 3s
250 Deployable Spawn a temporary sandbag or ammo crate
400 Special Class-specific temporary ability

Cost curve matters: 400 valor buys either two Overcharges or one Deployable. Tokens are the scarce resource, not money — so a player who spends early is weak late in the match.

7. Faction asymmetry

National and Royal weapons are not stat-swapped variants. The original's roster has perfect 173/173 symmetry, which is a design dead end — same numbers, different names.

We keep the symmetry of availability but break the symmetry of behaviour:

  • National weapons — high handling, lower range, faster recovery, more utility. Grassy, improvised, forgiving. Utility leans on the original's own kit: Burning Bullets, Blasting Strike, Poisoned Blade.
  • Royal weapons — precision, reach, heavier handling, higher burst damage. Institutional, deliberate, punishing to use badly. Utility leans on suppression and control: Hero Shield, Leg It, Frenzy Fire.

A National rifle and a Royal rifle of the same archetype differ in falloff shape, not falloff amount. Two players at the same level with the same Doctrine still fight differently because their tools behave differently.

Doctrine interaction. Each weapon declares which doctrine families it interacts with — some doctrines only work with close-range weapons, some with heavy. This makes loadouts cohere rather than being a bag of unrelated percentages.

8. Anti-patterns

  • Stat inflation. Upgrading a weapon should change behaviour, not just numbers. If Node 5 is "+8% damage", the weapon is still the same weapon — the tree failed.
  • Dominant branch. If one branch of Node 2 is strictly better, the branch is a fake choice. Every branch must be better in a different situation.
  • Respec as punishment. Respec must be affordable. A player who experiments should not be locked into a bad tree for a month.
  • Horizontal bloat. Nine weapons with identical function and different names is a worse roster than five weapons with distinct functions. The original had 346 and it was the problem, not the solution.
  • Signature dilution. If a signature is a flat bonus, it is a cosmetic tier. Each signature must change what the weapon does in a fight.
  • Paid power tiers. Super/Uber were bought with premium currency for "minor stat changes". In this design, no premium currency may purchase any stat. Premium buys cosmetics, Doctrine variants, and storage — nothing else.
